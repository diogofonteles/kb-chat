# 0036 — Access levels per collection: scales and item levels in the app's state, enforced by one viewer-scoped content view

- Status: accepted (2026-11-07: the owner approved every recommended default of KBC-103 to KBC-111 and of this ADR in conversation; proposed 2026-11-06), to be implemented by KBC-103 to KBC-111
- Amends: [0008](0008-google-login-and-permissions.md) (a person holds a level per collection besides collections and features), [0028](0028-app-owned-collections-and-sub-collections.md) §5 ("access follows the tree" now also says *how deep* into a collection), [0003](0003-facets-from-frontmatter-catalog.md) (the catalog carries an app-owned level per entry; counts and facets come from the viewer's view), [0022](0022-media-library-store-queue-and-streaming.md) (a `media.level` column), [0026](0026-offline-media-downloads.md) (a new revoke reason `level`), [0035](0035-tracks-as-kb-documents.md) §5 (the `media.db` visibility check of a track document includes its level)
- Keeps: Q7 (the API never writes to the vault or the QMD index); one access guard per route (ADR 0008); masters see everything
- Drivers: the owner's request of 2026-11-06 (`work/LEVELS/request.md`: per collection, a master registers ordered levels such as QS < CI < CDC < QM < QM+; a higher level sees everything at or below it; once a collection has levels, whoever inserts a document or audio must choose one; "as pesquisas só poderão pesquisar em conteúdos que estejam de acordo com o seu nível"); confidentiality (a new driver: no title, snippet, count or citation of content above a person's level may reach them); Q1/Q2 latency; Q7

## Context

Access today is one decision per route: may this person see this collection (`canSeeTopic`, `person.topics`, widened from a parent to its sub-collections). After the guard every service trusts the whole collection: the catalog (`CatalogRegistry`, one in-memory map per collection, shared by everyone), the search post-filter (`keepCataloged` + `refine`, top 60/200 from QMD), facets and suggestions over every entry, "Veja também" and relation pages, the research tools (bound to a collection name, never to a viewer: `ResearchToolsFactory = (topic) => …`, `resolveEntry` accepts any id of the collection), the sweep's target set, `TextFinder`'s folded corpus, the media library (`isVisible`), "Na fala" (FTS5 joined to `media` by `scopeClause`), single media items (`MediaService.visible`), stored conversations (answers and sources re-served with no check) and the upload duplicate answer (title of the matching item).

Documents are produced by `kb` on the owner's Mac and mirrored with `rsync --delete` (ADR 0010); the API cannot write them (Q7). There is no document upload in the app (KBC-009 is a draft); "inserting a document" is the owner adding it to the vault. Media is uploaded in the app into `media.db` (ADR 0022, 0033); tracks are rows of `media` with `parent_media_id` (KBC-068); track documents (ADR 0035) are proposed and not built.

QMD 2.8.3's `query` tool filters only by `collections` (OR) and `minScore`; it cannot filter by a per-document attribute. A QMD collection is a folder plus a glob mask, built on the Mac.

## Decision

1. **A scale per top-level collection, in the app's state.** `access-levels.json` in the state directory (atomic writes, zod-validated, loaded at start and kept in memory, like `collections.json` and `authors.json`): per top-level collection an ordered list of levels `{ id, code, description?, createdBy, createdAt }`, lowest first. `id` comes from the code (lowercase, no accents, `+` → `-plus`, e.g. `qm-plus`) and is fixed forever; `code` (≤ 12 characters, unique in the scale) and `description` can be renamed; the order is the list's order. A sub-collection uses its parent's scale, found through the catalog's tree, never by the slug's text (ADR 0028 §5, ADR 0030 §2). A collection with no levels behaves exactly as today.

2. **A person's level is on the person.** `storedPersonSchema` gains `levels?: Record<topLevelSlug, levelId>`; invite presets gain the same map. In a collection with levels, a person with access and no entry (or an entry naming a level that no longer exists) holds the **lowest** level. Masters hold every level implicitly (no entry needed). While a master acts as a person (ADR 0027), the person's level applies.

3. **An item's level lives beside the item's other app-owned state.**
   - **Vault documents:** `document-levels.json` in the state directory: per collection slug (the document's own collection, sub-collection included), `docId → { level, setBy, setAt }`. Never in the vault, and the frontmatter is never read for a level: the vault is mirrored with `--delete`, `kb` does not own such a key, and a permission must be changed and audited in the app. The catalog joins the map when it builds an entry (`CatalogEntry.level: levelId | 'unassigned'`, recomputed on a level write without re-reading the vault).
   - **Media and tracks:** `media.level TEXT NULL` (expand-only migration). A track copies its recording's level when it is created and may hold its own afterwards. Moving an item to another top-level collection requires a level of the destination's scale in the same request.
   - **Track documents (ADR 0035):** their level is the track's, read from `media.db` in the visibility check 0035 §5 already makes; it is never rendered into the vault (a level change must not republish).

4. **When a scale is first created, every existing item of the collection (and its sub-collections) gets the lowest level and every person with access gets the lowest level,** written in the same operation and audited as one bulk event. Nothing changes for anyone until a master raises something. The owner chose this default on 2026-11-07 (the alternative, existing content at the highest level, fail-closed, was not taken).

5. **Inserting requires a level; content without one is closed.** In a collection with levels, creating a media item without `level` is refused (`LEVEL_REQUIRED`, 400); the uploader chooses among the levels at or below their own (masters: any). A vault document that appears after the scale exists has no entry, so it is `unassigned`: visible only to masters and to people with the new feature `content-levels` at the top level, listed in "Sem nível" until someone assigns it. A media row with `level IS NULL` in a collection with levels (rows from before the migration that the bulk seed missed) is also `unassigned`.

6. **The visibility rule, in one function.** `allows(viewer, item) = isMaster(viewer) || (canSeeTopic(viewer, item.topic) && (scaleOf(top(item.topic)) is empty || (item.level ≠ 'unassigned' && rank(item.level) ≤ rank(levelOf(viewer, top(item.topic))))))`. Ranks are positions in the current list, never stored on items, so a reorder needs no data migration.

7. **One viewer-scoped content view, the only way to content.** A new module `access-levels` exposes `ContentGate.viewFor(viewer, topic): LevelView` with `allows(entry)`, `allowedLevelIds(top)`, `entries(scope)` and `visibleShare(collection)`. Every service that reveals content receives a `LevelView`, not the raw registry:
   - `CatalogRegistry` keeps building per collection, shared by everyone (it is content, not an answer); `LevelView.entries()` is the filtered set, memoized per `(collection, allowed level set, catalog version, levels version)`.
   - **Set-based surfaces filter before they compute** (true pre-filter): facets, topic counts, suggestions, `list_sources`, `list_subjects`, `find_text`/grep (the corpus is shared, the document set handed to it is the view's), `read_document(s)`, `related_documents`, the sweep's `targetSet` and coverage, "Veja também" and relation pages, the library, bookmarks, the processing queue, voices.
   - **SQL surfaces filter in the query:** "Na fala", library search, subjects → tracks and counts add `m.level IN (<allowed ids of that collection>)` to `scopeClause`/`liveTrackClause` per collection; `tally` and `count` come from the same filtered query.
   - **QMD ranking is filtered at the single point where index hits become catalog entries** (`keepCataloged` takes the view), before grouping, snippets, copies, totals, `capped` or facets are computed, with an **adaptive candidate budget**: `limit = min(1000, ceil(base / visibleShare))` (`base` = 60, or 200 with facet filters; `visibleShare` = visible entries / entries of that collection, ≥ 0.05), and one deeper call (× 4, same cap) when a page comes back short while the index returned a full `limit`. Copies of a group are only the visible ones; a group whose every member is hidden disappears.
   - **Single items** answer 404 (`DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `MEDIA_NOT_FOUND`) for an item the viewer may not see, exactly like an item that does not exist (no 403 that would confirm it exists).
   - **Research and MCP:** `ResearchToolsFactory` becomes `(topic, view) => …`; the turn's MCP token (lent Claude, Codex runner) maps to tools built with the turn's view, so an external model reaches only what the person reaches.

8. **Stored and client-sent content is re-checked at read.** A stored research answer keeps its prose; each source the viewer may no longer see is served as `{ restricted: true }` with no id, title, authorship or passage, and the turn carries `restrictedSources: n`. A sweep cursor's findings and a follow-up's cited ids are re-validated against the view at the start of the next turn; hidden findings are dropped and the coverage recounted.

9. **Who does what.** Creating, renaming, reordering and deleting levels: masters only (new master-only feature `collection-levels`). A person's level: masters, in the person editor, invite presets and the access-request approval. An item's level: the uploader at upload and whoever may edit the item (`canEditMedia`), masters and holders of `content-levels` for vault documents; never above their own level (masters: any). Level names: a member sees only the codes at or below their own (the upload choices); badges with levels appear only to masters and holders of `content-levels`.

10. **Changes after the fact.** Reorder: the new order applies at once to every item and person, after a confirmation that shows how many people gain and lose how many items. Delete: refused while items or people hold the level unless the request names a target level for items (default: the next higher, fail-closed) and one for people (default: the next lower, least privilege), applied atomically. Deleting the last level returns the collection to "no levels" after a typed confirmation. Each change takes effect on the next request (people and level caches are updated in-process on write; the 60 s `PeopleService` cache is invalidated by the write).

11. **Audit.** Every write above appends to `audit/levels-YYYY-MM.jsonl` (append-only, 0600, 12 months, like act-as): who (and who acted as whom), when, what (`scale.create|rename|reorder|delete`, `person.level`, `item.level`, `bulk`), the collection, before → after and, for bulk, the count and the filter. Masters read it in "Histórico de níveis". The audit file cannot join `media.db`'s transaction, so a media level is written first and recorded right after (KBC-105): if the record fails, the API logs `media level changed but not audited` (or `media levels seeded but not audited`) with the id and before → after and fails the request; a retry finds the level already set and records nothing, so that log line is the only trace and is reconciled by hand.

12. **Executable rules.** A boundary test fails CI when a module other than `catalog`, `access-levels` and the composition root imports `CatalogRegistry.get/scope` or the media store's list methods directly; a leakage suite (KBC-111) runs every content route with two people at different levels and asserts that no title, id, snippet, count or citation of a hidden item appears in any response or SSE event.

## Amendment (KBC-107, 2026-11-11)

- **Presence of vault documents:** `document-presence.json` in the state directory, apart from `document-levels.json` (so an API rolled back to the previous image still reads that file's strict schema): per collection and document id, `firstSeenAt` (when a document without a level was first seen in a collection with a scale: "chegou em") and `missingSince` (when a document with a level stopped being in the vault). It is reconciled after every catalog load; a level whose document has been missing for more than 30 days is dropped (§5: the document comes back as "Sem nível").
- **`content-levels`** ("Classificar nível de conteúdo", granted per person): its holder's view also admits the documents without a level of the collections they see and shows the badges (§9); they give documents a level at or below their own, singly or in bulk (`POST …/document-levels`, by ids or by the "Sem nível" filter as of the list's `asOf`). Unassigned media stays with masters.
- **Audit:** a bulk is one `bulk` line with the filter (or `{ ids: n }`) and the counts, plus one `item.level` line per document, written in one append and one sync.

## Amendment (KBC-109, 2026-11-11)

- **Presets carry levels:** an invitation preset (and so an invite profile) has an optional `levels` map, absent when empty so a profile without levels stays readable by an older image. A level must name a top-level collection with a scale that the preset's collections reach, by the tree (a sub-collection reaches its parent), and one of its levels: the API checks it (400 `VALIDATION_FAILED`), since the contract cannot see the tree; a master preset holds none.
- **One resolution for every grant:** an invitation, "Aplicar perfil" and a change of a person's collections (the path after an approved access request) write, for each collection with a scale they reach, the level named when it exists, else the person's valid level when the preset does not name that collection, else the lowest. Collections not reached keep their level. A profile's level that no longer exists counts as the lowest and the profile read marks it in `staleLevels`.
- **People in bulk:** `POST /api/v1/people/levels` (masters) sets one level of one collection for the people named or for everyone, in one write of `people.json`, inside the levels write queue; masters, people who do not reach the collection and unknown ids are left as they are and counted. Audit: one `bulk` line (`count.people`, `count.skipped`, `filter: { ids: n }`) and one `person.level` line per person changed, in one append.
- **Where a level was set:** `person.level` and the people `bulk` lines carry `via` (`editor`, `invite`, `profile`, `bulk`); an older image drops the key when it reads the audit.
- **One queue, the rule everywhere (review round 1):** an invitation, "Aplicar perfil", a person's change and the people bulk read the scale and the person, write `people.json` and append their audit inside the levels write queue, so a first scale or a bulk never slips between them and each line's `before` is the previous line's `after`. A person's change refuses levels on whoever is or becomes a master (400). Becoming a master (by the editor or a master profile) drops the levels held, audited (`after: null`); a master made member gets the lowest level of each collection with a scale they reach, audited, and never a level from before. Undoing a profile or an invitation the house AI could not follow runs in its own turn of the queue: the person is read again, a level written meanwhile (a bulk) stays, and the undo is audited from what is there now; the client always gets the AI's failure, and a failed undo is logged as such. Known limits, accepted: an undo cannot tell a bulk that wrote the same level the profile gave (that level goes back too), and it restores the fields other than levels as they were before the profile.
- **A profile names a level or keeps the person's:** in the profile editor a collection the profile names no level for shows "Manter o nível da pessoa" (the API's "not named"); renaming a profile or marking it the default sends no preset, so it never writes levels. Without the scales (loading or failed) a profile's levels are kept as they came, and saving waits for them when they failed. A level that no longer exists asks for a choice before the preset is saved; applying such a profile still gives the lowest level there (BR-3, least privilege), and the confirmation says so.
- **Invite profiles read tolerantly:** `invite-profiles.json` loads with the features this image does not know left out, with a warning, as `people.json` since KBC-107.

## Amendment (KBC-108, 2026-11-11)

- **The scale's version:** `GET …/levels` answers `version`, a digest (sha256, 16 hex) of the scale's levels in order (id, code, description). Every write of the scale changes it; no field is added to `access-levels.json`, whose strict schema an image rolled back (ADR 0010) must still read. A scale written back exactly as it was has its old version again, and a preview of it holds again (accepted: the operation acts on the state of now, so nobody gains access by it; review R1-7).
- **Preview, then apply (§10):** `POST …/levels/preview` takes a new order, the removal of one level or every level (`{ all: true }`), with the version the master saw, and answers counts only: how many people gain or lose sight of how many documents and audios (people grouped by the level they hold, items by theirs; never a title, an id or a name), for a removal what the level holds and its default targets (content: the next higher, or the next lower for the highest; people: the next lower, or the next higher for the lowest), and how many invite profiles name the level (or any level, for all) and so will read as `staleLevels` (KBC-109). Whoever holds no level of the scale (none recorded, or one deleted since) sits at the lowest level of whatever the scale becomes: the preview counts them there before and after (review R1-1). `PATCH …/levels` (reorder), `DELETE …/levels/:level` and `DELETE …/levels` require the version of the preview: another one answers 409 `LEVELS_CHANGED`, and T24 counts again on the scale of now before letting anyone confirm (R1-2).
- **Delete with targets, all or nothing:** `DELETE …/levels/:level` `{ moveContentTo, movePeopleTo, version }` runs in the levels write queue as steps that each return their undo: `access-levels.json` first (from then on no upload, media edit, person or document write can choose the leaving level: media validates and writes in one synchronous run, people's and documents' level writes wait in the same queue, R1-4), then `media.db` (one transaction), `document-levels.json`, `people.json` (whoever holds the level, and, when it is the lowest, whoever reaches the collection with no level of the scale), a second pass over `media.db` for a row written meanwhile, then the audit. A failure in any step, the audit included, undoes the earlier ones in reverse order (the scale back last; meanwhile its items are fail-closed) and fails the request. An undo puts back only what still holds the value the step wrote: a person, an audio or a document changed by another path meanwhile keeps that change and the log says `level change undo left a write made meanwhile` (R1-3). An undo that fails is logged (`level change rolled back incompletely`) with the collection, the total and the first 50 ids with their levels before (R1-5). The only level is not deleted this way (400): it goes with every level.
- **Every level away:** `DELETE …/levels` `{ confirm: <slug>, version }`: `media.level` set to NULL, the scope's entries of `document-levels.json` and `document-presence.json` removed (no write when there is none, so an undo never leaves a file that was not there, QA-3), the collection's key removed from every person's `levels`, the scale removed from `access-levels.json` (after the items: removed first, an undo would open everything while it puts levels back), a second pass over `media.db`, then the audit. Invite profiles that name a deleted level are not rewritten: they read as `staleLevels` (KBC-109).
- **A level id is never given again while something names it (QA-2):** a new level's id is the code's, with `-2`, `-3`… when the scale has it or an invite profile or a person still names it in the collection; so a profile left on a deleted "CI" keeps asking for a choice after a new "CI". No file changes; an older image reads the ids as before.
- **Audit:** `scale.delete` (before: the level's code and description; after: the targets; the counts) and `scale.clear` (before: the ids in order; after: their ids and codes; the counts), each preceded in the same append by one `item.level` line per document and audio and one `person.level` line per person moved (`via: scale`), so the history shows what to redo by hand (there is no automatic undo). `count.people` is every entry of `people.json` rewritten: the people with access the preview counted, and an entry left by someone who lost the collection since (R1-14). When the append fails, a `scale.undone` line (the top-level collection and the operation's moment) is written on a line of its own (a newline first, so a torn last line cannot swallow it), best effort; if it fails too, the log names the operation (`at`, `by`, kind). The history leaves out every line of that operation, its sub-collections' documents and audios included (R1-5, R2-1, R2-4).
- **"Histórico de níveis":** `GET /api/v1/levels/audit?topic&actor&kind&target&from&to&cursor&limit` (masters, `collection-levels`; a member gets 403) reads the monthly files newest first, a month only when the page still needs lines, with an opaque cursor (month and line); a line that does not parse is skipped. Each page also reads the scale's own lines (create, rename, delete, clear, undone) of every month, for the codes of deleted levels and the undone marks: the adapter keeps only those lines of each month parsed (until its file's size or mtime changes) and at most two whole months, so a page parses only what changed and the memory stays bounded (R2-5, R3-2). Every write starts on a fresh line, so a torn last line never takes the next one with it (R3-3); a month file is removed once a write is twelve months past it, which keeps the 12 months the port promises (R3-2). `from`/`to` take instants (T24 sends the browser's local day, R1-9) or UTC days. Lines are served with names and codes (the person's name, the item's title, level codes; a deleted level's code from the scale's own lines, read whole; failing that, its id in capitals, R1-8).

## Amendment (KBC-106, 2026-11-11)

- **A track's own level (§3):** `media.level` stays the track's effective level and a new `media.level_own` (migration 33, expand only) says it no longer follows its recording; the draft keeps the level chosen in the validation step in `media_draft_tracks.level` (NULL: the recording's). A track's level is never below its recording's (400 `LEVEL_BELOW_RECORDING`) nor above the editor's own (403 `LEVEL_ABOVE_YOURS`); choosing the recording's level is following it. `PATCH /media/:track` takes `level` (null: back to the recording's) and writes the draft row of the same track, so the next approval keeps it; an approval applies each track's drafted level to its item at once. Every change of an item's level is one `item.level` line (`count.tracks: 1`).
- **Following the recording (KBC-105 BR-7, KBC-106 BR-1):** a recording's new level moves the tracks that follow it; a track of its own below the new level goes up to it and stays its own, so it never comes down when the recording does (least privilege). In another top-level collection every track takes the new level and follows again. A scale taken away clears `level_own`.
- **Visibility:** tracks answer by their own level everywhere a recording does (`seesMedia`, the search's `m.level IN (…)`, proposals, moved bookmarks); a track document's level is read from media.db by `LevelView.allows` (the gate's `mediaLevelOf`), so every surface that already asks the view (tools, sweep, "Veja também", suggestions) and the shelf agree. The collection's subject and speaker lists, and the search's subject filters, stay masters' in a collection with levels (`listsClosedByLevels`, `LEVELS_PENDING`): a list across all of a collection's media is not filtered here.
- **Hidden parts of a recording (FR-2):** for whoever hears a recording but not one of its live tracks, `MediaItem.hidden` lists those tracks' real bounds (merged where they touch). The stream sends every AAC frame that touches a hidden part as a silent frame of the same size (one SCE with no spectral data, padded with fill elements), found through the MP4 sample table (`stts`, `stsc`, `stsz`, `stco`/`co64`, the edit list's start), so the file stays playable and seekable in every browser and nothing hidden leaves the server; a `Range` wholly inside a hidden part is 416; the entity tag carries a digest of the hidden set; a file whose sample table cannot be read is not served to that person (409 `MEDIA_NOT_READY`). Measured before choosing: truncating the response before the part and answering 416 from it left WebKit retrying in a loop and never playing. The transcript keeps a hidden segment in its place with no text and `restricted: true` (word positions and corrections do not move); words, learned spans, subtitles, "Na fala" and the library's speech search leave out every segment or word that touches a hidden part (fail-closed). The player skips each part, announced as "Trecho restrito pulado.", and hatches it on the bar with "Trecho restrito".
- **Import from the Mac (ADR 0038):** `media.level_own` and `media_draft_tracks.level` belong to production like `media.level`: out of the unit's digest, never overwritten by a later import (a draft's level production gave survives a replacement), and a draft's level from the Mac arrives only when production's scale has it (else the recording's).

## Options considered

1. **Scales, person levels and item levels in the app's state; one viewer-scoped view (chosen).** Works on the read-only vault (Q7), survives every sync, changes take effect at once and are audited in one place. Cost: the app holds a second fact about each vault document, and new documents start closed until classified.
2. **Level as a frontmatter key written by `kb` on the Mac.** Keeps the level next to the content and lets the owner bulk-edit in files, but every change needs the Mac, a sync and a restart; a sync with `--delete` and a mistyped key silently opens or closes content; nothing is audited; and media still needs the app. Not a seed either: the owner approved the default on 2026-11-07 (levels are set only in the app); revisit only if a one-time seed from files is asked for.
3. **One QMD collection per (collection, level), built on the Mac.** The only way to filter inside QMD; it would need the Mac to know the levels (an export job, as in ADR 0035) and a vault layout or masks per level, and every level change would wait for a re-index. Revisit if the recall NFR of KBC-111 fails with the adaptive budget.
4. **Read QMD's SQLite index directly with an allowlist join.** True in-query filter for lexical mode, but couples the API to QMD's internal schema (Q5) and does not cover vector search.
5. **Post-filter with the fixed 60/200 budget.** Simple, but a member who sees 10 % of a collection would get short or empty pages; rejected for recall.
6. **Levels as collections (one sub-collection per level).** Reuses today's checks, but breaks "a higher level sees everything below" (it would need a grant per level), forces content to move between folders on a level change and collides with real sub-collections.

## Consequences

- Good: a collection without levels is untouched; the first scale changes nothing until a master raises content; every surface goes through one rule and one view, with SQL and set surfaces filtered before they compute.
- Good: changes and their audit are immediate and in the app; external models (lent Claude, Codex) inherit the person's view through the turn's tools.
- Bad: QMD ranking is still post-filtered; with very small visible shares the adaptive budget costs latency (bounded by the 1000 cap) and may lose recall at the tail; KBC-111 measures it.
- Bad: one more pair of state files to back up together with `collections.json` and `authors.json`; `document-levels.json` grows with the vault (≈ 100 bytes per document: 10 k documents ≈ 1 MB).
- Bad: a document the owner adds on the Mac stays invisible to members until classified in the app; "Sem nível" and its count make that visible to masters.
- Risk: a future surface that reads the registry directly leaks; the boundary test and the leakage suite are the guard. Codex in local mode (no runner URL) reads the filesystem with a best-effort sandbox; levels do not change that development-only exposure, and production uses the runner (ADR 0020).
- Compliance: unit tests of the rule, ranks, defaults and the adaptive budget; integration tests per surface with two viewers; migration test of `media.level` over today's database; E2E of the levels page, the person editor and the upload field; the leakage suite and the recall/latency script of KBC-111.

## Owner decisions (2026-11-07)

The owner approved in conversation every default the items and this ADR had assumed: existing content and people without a level start at the lowest level (§4); a new vault document stays closed until classified, with no per-collection default level (§5, KBC-107); a sub-collection uses its parent's scale (§1); members see no level badges (§9); an uploader cannot choose a level above their own (§9, KBC-105); a track inherits its recording's level and may hold a higher one (§3, KBC-106); deletion targets default to the next higher level for content and the next lower for people (§10, KBC-108); a stored answer keeps its prose and only restricted sources are masked (§8, KBC-110); no frontmatter seed (option 2). The status is accepted.
