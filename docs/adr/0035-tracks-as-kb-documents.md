# 0035 — Transcribed tracks become kb documents: written and classified by `kb` on the owner's Mac, cited to the millisecond through `media.db`

- Status: accepted (2026-11-07, the owner's decisions; proposed 2026-11-05; amended 2026-11-06 by the KBC-099 spike on kb 0.0.12 and its review, and 2026-11-07 by the owner: track documents are part of the collection's document search, see "Amendment 2026-11-07")
- Supersedes: [0024](0024-media-search-outside-the-vault.md) §4 ("not in the vault, for now"; phase C as tools over the FTS5 index)
- Amends: [0023](0023-transcription-pull-worker-on-the-owners-mac.md) (a new job kind; the worker writes into the vault on the Mac), [0029](0029-recording-edits-speakers-tracks-and-subjects.md) option 5 (accepted subjects are rendered into the track's document; `media.db` stays their authority), [0021](0021-app-driven-exhaustive-sweep.md) (tracks are documents of the sweep's set; coverage names them), [0010](0010-deploy-oracle-cloud-always-free.md) (the vault sync runs by itself and the index swap stops being an API restart)
- Keeps: Q7 (the API never writes to the vault or the QMD index); 0024 §1–§3 (the FTS5 index still serves "Na fala", the Library search and exact times)
- Drivers: the owner's request of 2026-11-05 (KBC-098: "cada transcrição de faixa deve virar um documento do acervo … pesquisável através de uma conversa"; "a mesma classificação que fazemos ao adicionar um acervo (texto) … este deve ser o padrão"; complement: "usar o kb para fazer o index e classificacao"); Q4 grounding; Q3 cost; Q7; Q1 latency

## Context

Every research tool (`search_collection`, `find_text`, `read_document(s)`, `list_sources`, `related_documents`), the exhaustive sweep (`SweepPlanner.targetSet` = `catalog.get(topic).entries`), its coverage (a prefix of document ids) and the `SourceBook` work on one unit: a vault Markdown file keyed by its topic-relative path, described by the frontmatter `kb classify` and `kb link` write (ADR 0003) and found through QMD (ADR 0002). Authorship comes from `author_group` (ADR 0015), written by the owner's `mark-authorship.py`, a key kb never rewrites.

Transcripts live in `media.db` (ADR 0022/0023): segments and words in milliseconds, one current version per recording, and per published track a derived slice on the track's own clock (`TrackPublisher.toDerived`, KBC-068). ADR 0024 §4 kept them out of the vault and planned a "phase C" of research tools over FTS5. No backlog item for phase C exists; the research agent does not see a single track.

The owner now asks for the opposite of 0024 §4: a track is a document of the collection, classified the same way a text is, by `kb`, by default. Before this request the vault already held lectures transcribed outside the app and ingested with `kb ingest file` (`source_path: …/audio/…`, `genre: talk_or_interview`): the catalog marks them `origin: audio`. 0024 itself said to revisit "if the owner wants transcripts inside `kb`".

The two objections 0024 raised against option 2 (a Markdown copy in the vault) were: timing is lost in Markdown, and uploads would wait for a manual `kb ingest` and `sync-vault.sh`. Q7 forbids the **API** from writing the vault; the vault is produced by `kb` on the Mac (ADR 0010), where the transcription worker already runs (ADR 0023).

## Decision

1. **One document per published track, in the vault, written on the Mac.** Each ready, non-archived track (KBC-068) becomes `<topic>/raw/media/<recordingId>/<trackId>.md` in the collection's vault folder (a sub-collection uses its own folder). A transcribed recording without tracks becomes one document for the whole recording (`<recordingId>/<recordingId>.md`). The API never writes the file: it renders the Markdown and hands it to the Mac in a job; the Mac writes it. Q7 holds.

2. **The rendered document** (deterministic, renderer version `kb_chat_render: 1`):
   - Frontmatter the app owns (kb leaves keys it does not own untouched, as with `author_group`): `title` (track title), `type: source`, `stage: raw`, `source_kind: media-transcript`, `source_path: media/<topic>/<recordingId>/<trackId>`, `domain` (the collection's `topic.yaml` domain), `scraped` (the date of the transcript in use, never the clock, so the render stays deterministic), `tags` (`[<domain>, raw, media-transcript]`), `author_group` (`mestre-laercio | outros-autores`, from the media authorship, ADR 0015), `author` (the author's name, ADR 0030), `kb_chat_media_id`, `kb_chat_media_kind: track | recording`, `kb_chat_recording_id`, `kb_chat_recording_title`, `kb_chat_track_ordinal`, `kb_chat_tracks_total`, `kb_chat_start_ms` (the track's start in the recording), `kb_chat_duration_ms`, `kb_chat_confidence: normal | low`, `kb_chat_subjects` (accepted KBC-069 subjects, names), `kb_chat_fingerprint` (sha256 of the rendered document before kb touches it) and `kb_chat_render`.
   - Body: `# <track title>`, one line "Gravação: <recording> · faixa n de N · <author> · <duration>", then paragraphs that each start with a time anchor on the track's clock, `[m:ss]` or `[h:mm:ss]`, followed by the text of **one segment** (amended in KBC-099: one paragraph per segment, not 600-character paragraphs, so every passage the agent may cite has its own anchor and a citation lands on exactly one `media.db` segment). A named speaker (KBC-065) prefixes the paragraph ("**Fulano:**"). Anchors are seconds; milliseconds are never needed in the file (§6). Speech is written on one line; a time said in brackets becomes `(m:ss)` and a spoken `[[x]]` becomes `[ [x] ]`, so neither reads as an anchor or a wikilink; the speaker's name gets the same treatment.
   - `domain`, `scraped` and `tags` exist because `kb lint` requires them of a raw document (the spike: without them every media document is a lint error).
   - Fields `kb classify` and `kb link` own (`genre`, `depth`, `relevance`, `quality`, `concepts`, `summary`, `entities`, `questions`, relations such as `example_of`) are left for kb to write. kb 0.0.12 appends them at the end of the frontmatter and never changes the app's keys or the body (spike). **Kept on rewrite:** when the Mac rewrites a document, it carries over every key of the file on disk that is neither the app's (the list above, `kb_chat_*`) nor in the new render. Needed because classify and link skip a document whose body did not change (`unchanged`): a render that changes only the frontmatter (subjects, render version) would otherwise lose its facets for good; when the body did change, kb judges it again and replaces those keys in place (spike).

3. **The same `kb` steps a text gets, by default.** A new job kind `kb-publish` is leased only by a paired transcriber marked **Publica no acervo** (like "Separa vozes", KBC-064), configured with the vault path on the Mac. A job carries up to 50 documents of one collection (`write { path, markdown }` or `remove { path }`). The worker takes a lock file in the vault, writes and removes the files, then runs, for that topic, `kb classify <topic> --budget <usd>`, `kb index --topic <topic>`, `kb link <topic> --budget <usd>` and `kb index --topic <topic>` again (all incremental: unchanged documents cost nothing; indexing before link gives link its vector neighbours, the second index takes link's keys; amended by the spike, which found that `kb index` takes no `--budget`: it is local and spends nothing). The steps are **topic-wide**: a job also pays for any other changed document of the topic kb had not judged yet. A run that reaches its budget exits 0 but names the documents it left `undecided`: those are reported as not published (`ok: false`) and come back in the next job, which resumes through kb's cache. kb's links in the body exist only with `decisions.mode: apply`; the owner's vault is in `shadow` (kb's default), so link only writes frontmatter relations, and what `apply` would do to the `[m:ss]` paragraphs was not verified. It reports per document: written path, `kb` exit status, the kb facets it now has, and the spend kb's receipts record. `kb ingest` is not used: its fetch, conversion and gates (dedupe, relevance, quality) do not apply to a track a person approved, and ingesting again would duplicate the document. Classification is **automatic, with no person confirming** (the owner's "este deve ser o padrão"); a track off the topic's contract goes to kb's own review queue, as a text does, and stays a document.

4. **Fresh by reconciliation, not by callbacks.** The API keeps `media_documents (media_id, topic, path, desired_fingerprint, published_fingerprint, published_at, state, last_error, attempts)`. A reconciler renders every publishable track (cheap: text from `media.db`) after each change callback that exists today (`afterTranscript`, `afterRecordingEdit`, track revision, archive, restore, subjects, and the KBC-093 correction transaction when it lands) and every 10 minutes as a backstop. A different fingerprint enqueues a `write`; a track that stopped being publishable (archived, deleted, recording unpublished, moved to another collection, the collection has no vault folder any more) enqueues a `remove` at its old path. Jobs coalesce: one pending job per collection at a time; a newer fingerprint replaces the payload of a queued job. A re-transcription, a word correction, a bound or title edit, a new speaker name, an authorship change, a subject change or a move all change the render, so all of them republish, reclassify and reindex through the same path.

5. **The vault is still the content; `media.db` is the visibility.** The catalog parses `kb_chat_*` keys into `CatalogEntry.media` (`{ mediaId, kind, recordingId, recordingTitle, startMs, durationMs, ordinal, total, confidence, fingerprint }`) and sets `origin: audio`. Before any research tool, the sweep, the T2 document group, facet counts or "Veja também" uses an entry with `media`, the API checks `media.db`: the item exists, is ready, not archived, still in this collection, and the viewer may hear media there (`media`) and has `media-tracks` and `media-research` (amended in KBC-099: `media-tracks`, the permission to see tracks at all, is required too, four in all with the collection's `research`). A track removed in the app disappears from research at once, even before the Mac removes the file. A stale document (published fingerprint ≠ desired) is still searchable, labelled "atualizando" to the agent and in the source chip, until the new one arrives.

6. **Citations carry the track and the milliseconds.** Tool outputs show a track document with its kind ("faixa de áudio, transcrição automática"), recording, ordinal, author and authorship, and keep the anchors in every window. The agent cites a passage of a track as `[n@m:ss]` (plain `[n]` stays valid). `answerSourceSchema` grows additively: `media? { mediaId, kind, recordingTitle, ordinal?, total?, durationMs, confidence }` and `passages? [{ at: "m:ss", startMs, endMs }]`. The API resolves each passage, in order: (a) the time written in the marker, accepted only if that anchor was shown to the model for that source in this turn, snapped to the `media.db` segment that contains it; (b) for a plain `[n]` or a rejected time, the last window or hit the tools showed for that source (character offset → the preceding anchor → its segment); (c) a sweep finding's quote, located in the body with wiki-link markup stripped. `startMs`/`endMs` are the segment's exact times from `media.db`, on the track's clock. Old answers keep rendering: the fields are optional.

7. **Opening a track citation opens the transcript, not the Markdown.** The web routes a source with `media` to a panel in the reading slot that shows the transcript from `media.db` (always the current version, with words for the spoken-word highlight, KBC-045) with the cited passage highlighted and scrolled into view, and the track's player beside it, positioned 2 s before `startMs`, paused, ready to play. The vault Markdown is never shown to people; it exists for kb and for the agent.

8. **Search outside the conversation does not change (until KBC-101; amended 2026-11-07).** Entries with `media` stay out of the T2 document group, its facet counts and the document panel's "Veja também" until KBC-101 shows them there as tracks (see "Amendment 2026-11-07"); "Na fala", the Library search (KBC-096) and subjects (KBC-069/070) keep reading `media.db` as before.

9. **Publishing to production.** In KBC-099 the owner's existing `sync-vault.sh` publishes the documents. In KBC-100 the worker runs the sync by itself after a publish batch (at most once every 30 minutes, none during a deploy, the existing lock and deletion cap), and `install-index.sh` gains a hot mode: the API restarts only its long-lived `qmd mcp` child on a trigger file, without stopping the API (live research turns, KBC-029, survive); the catalog already follows the vault through chokidar. The API learns that a document reached production when the catalog sees its `kb_chat_fingerprint`; only then is the track "na pesquisa com IA".

10. **When the Mac is off.** `kb-publish` jobs wait in the queue like `transcribe` (lease 60 s, heartbeat, 4 attempts, then `dead` with the error kept). The track page, to people with `media-edit`, and Transcritores, to masters, show the state: "Na pesquisa com IA", "Aguardando o Mac do dono (desligado desde …)", "Atualizando na pesquisa", "Falhou: <motivo>" with **Tentar de novo**. Resuming needs nothing: the worker leases the queue when it comes back. Phase 3 (ADR 0031) may later run the same steps on the server; the job contract does not change.

11. **Cost and policy.** `kb classify` and `kb link` spend on the owner's kb key on the Mac (OpenRouter: the spike measured US$ 0.0007–0.0010 per track's first publication, classify ≈ US$ 0.0004–0.0006 and link ≈ US$ 0.0002–0.0005, about US$ 0.001 per track; `kb index` costs nothing), not on the house plan (ADR 0016) or anyone's AI. The worker sums kb's receipt lines (`decisions:` and `generation:`, never the `budget:` line that echoes the cap) and reports the spend per document, and also on a failed job (a step that spent before a later one failed): the API books both on the media spend ledger, already settled, so the monthly media ceiling (US$ 10, plan v3) stops new publications with "teto mensal da mídia" shown, never silently. Each job passes `--budget` = the remaining ceiling, capped at US$ 0.50, to classify and link only; kb may pass it by one call (the spike spent US$ 0.0005 on a US$ 0.0003 budget). Research over tracks spends exactly as research over texts does, under the same per-person policy and quotas; no AI default appears in production.

## Options considered

1. **Vault document written and classified by kb on the Mac (chosen).** The owner's words, one search and classification path for texts and tracks, every research tool and the sweep for free, Q7 kept. Costs: the Mac is on the path (queue and status make it visible; ADR 0031 can move it), a minutes-long delay to production, a second copy of the text, kb's spend.
2. **Phase C as in 0024 §4: research tools over `media.db` FTS5.** No Mac, exact times, immediate. But not "the same classification as texts" (no kb facets, no `kb link` relations), every tool, the sweep, coverage and the catalog would need a second document source, and it contradicts the owner's explicit instruction to use kb.
3. **The API writes the document into the vault or the QMD index.** Breaks Q7 and ADR 0010 (the VM vault is read-only and is overwritten by every sync from the Mac).
4. **Run kb on the VM.** kb and the vault's source of truth live on the Mac; two writers to the vault would diverge; QMD embedding on the A1 VM is slow. Revisit with ADR 0031.
5. **`kb ingest file --force` for each track.** The literal "same as texts", but ingest converts and renames the file, re-ingesting an edited track creates a second document, and its gates are meant for unknown sources. Kept as the fallback if the KBC-099 spike finds that kb 0.0.12 does not classify a file it did not ingest.
6. **Human confirmation of kb's classification per track (as KBC-069).** Safer facets, but the owner asked for it by default; rejected by the owner on 2026-11-07.

## Consequences

- Good: the agent, every profile, the sweep and its coverage, authorship and the catalog see tracks with no new search stack; a track gets the same facets and relations as a text; the minute is exact because it comes from `media.db`.
- Good: one reconciliation covers every change, including KBC-093, without new hooks per feature.
- Bad: production lags the app by the sync interval (≤ 30 min with the Mac on), and stops while the Mac is off; the UI says so.
- Bad: two copies of the text (vault and `media.db`); the vault copy is derived and may be minutes stale, never shown to people.
- Bad: the worker now needs the vault path and the kb binary, and runs commands that spend money; it does so only when marked **Publica no acervo**.
- Risk: kb 0.0.12 is unstable (overview constraint); a change to how classify/link pick files or to frontmatter ownership breaks publishing. KBC-099 ran the spike on the real binary (2026-11-06, a scratch copy of the vault, US$ 0.0053): classify and link take a file written straight into `raw/media` without ingest and keep the app's keys and anchors; the worker's tests replay kb's real output (receipts, `undecided` lines, list indentation) with a fake kb (Q5).
- Compliance: unit tests of the renderer (deterministic, anchors, owner keys) and of passage resolution (a, b, c); integration tests of the reconciler (each change kind → write or remove), of the visibility filter (archived, other collection, no `media`, no `media-research`) and of the tools and citations on the fixture vault with track documents; the worker's tests with a fake kb; an E2E from question to citation to the panel with the player at −2 s.
- Revisit: when ADR 0031 moves processing off the Mac; if kb gains a library API; if the vault grows past 50k documents (ADR 0003).

## Amendment 2026-11-07 (owner's decisions)

The owner answered the open questions of KBC-098 in conversation. Approved as written: kb classifies and links tracks with no person confirming (§3); `media-research` is not granted by itself to people who hold `research` and `media`; the cited track's player opens paused at −2 s (§7); a transcribed recording without tracks becomes one document (KBC-100); low-confidence transcripts are searchable, labelled for the agent and in the source chip; the production sync runs at most every 30 minutes (§9).

Changed: **track documents are part of the collection's document search.** In the owner's words, "cada faixa transcrita é um «documento» do acervo": a visible track appears in the T2 document list, its facets (kb's `genre`, `depth`, `relevance`, `concepts`), its counts (summary, authorship, "Buscar em", chips) and "Mostrar mais", with the ♪ marker, and opens the T22 transcript panel with the player at the matched passage. This replaces the exclusion of §8 once KBC-101 ships. Rules (KBC-101 BR-6 to BR-11):

- Visibility is §5's `media.db` check done before facets, totals and pages are computed. T2 is not AI research, so it needs the collection, `media` and `media-tracks` (what already shows the track in the Library and "Na fala"), not `media-research`; research tools keep §5 as written. Access levels (ADR 0036 §3, KBC-106) apply to track entries here too.
- One card per track: a track document hit and a "Na fala" hit of the same track (or of a recording without tracks and its document) merge into one item before paging and counting, carrying the passage's time; "Na fala" hits of recordings whose tracks have no visible document stay as they are.
- In "Buscar em", tracks count under "Áudios"; tracks are never grouped as copies of a text or of another track.
- The search result item grows additively with `media? { mediaId, kind, recordingTitle, ordinal?, total?, startMs, confidence, stale }`.
