# 0029 — Recording edits: per-recording voices, named speakers, tracks approved by a person and then made derived media items cut by stream copy beside the original, and subject links, all in `media.db`

- Status: accepted (2026-11-03): the owner approved the recommended defaults of the three open decisions (below); the tools and models inside the decisions (diarization model, proposal model, thresholds) come from KBC-063 and do not reopen this ADR
- Amends: [0022](0022-media-library-store-queue-and-streaming.md) (new tables and job kinds `diarize`, `cut`, `voice-profile`), [0024](0024-media-search-outside-the-vault.md) (track items in the media index; concept filter on media)
- Drivers: the owner's request (2026-11-03): "receive an audio and edit it: identify the questions and answers and split them into tracks (the question together with the answer is one track), find the best title for each track; based on the knowledge in the system and on voice identification, already identify who is speaking or answering; at the end, everything goes through the collection's classification and links to related subjects, to make searching easier"; Q7 (the API never writes the vault); ADR 0023 (speech work only on the owner's Mac); the per-person feature rule (2026-10-28); the media monthly cap (US$ 10); LGPD (voice prints tied to a person are biometric, sensitive data)

## Context

Media items are single recordings with one transcript (ADR 0022, 0023) searchable in the app's own FTS5 index (ADR 0024). Lectures in the collection end with long question-and-answer sessions that people want to study one question at a time, find by subject, favourite, put in playlists and download (KBC-051 to KBC-056). The renditions are AAC 64 kbit/s mono; originals are not kept (owner's decision 2026-10-28), so whatever we derive must be re-creatable from the rendition, and the rendition must not be lost.

Three things are hard to reverse once data exists: how a track is represented (chapter metadata vs. its own media item), where voice data lives and for how long, and where subject links live (the vault vs. the app).

## Owner's decisions (2026-11-03)

"pode seguir com os padrões recomendados nas três decisões":

1. **Voice identification** only for living people who give specific, highlighted consent (purpose, retention, how to revoke), entered by a master; never audience members; deceased masters and authors only with written authorisation from whoever is responsible for the work (the institution or the heirs). Without consent, only manual names and context suggestions that do not use the voice.
2. **Tracks sit beside the original**; the full recording stays, grouped with its tracks. Hiding it from the library may come later, never deleting it.
3. **AI for tracks, titles and subjects**: a cheap paid model through the app's AI (ADR 0016), within the media monthly budget of US$ 10 (ADR 0022 §7), capped at US$ 0.25 per run, with the estimate shown before running; a local model on the Mac becomes the default (no cost) if KBC-063 finds one good enough.

## Decision

1. **Voices are per recording.** A `diarize` job runs on the owner's Mac worker (model chosen in KBC-063) and uploads speaker turns only (start, end, voice index). The API stores the raw result next to the transcript JSON and derives `media_speaker_turns` (media, transcript version, diarization version, voice, start ms, end ms) and the voice of each segment and word. **No voice embedding is stored** after the job; voice indices mean nothing outside that recording.
2. **Speakers are named by people.** `media_speakers` per collection (sub-collections use the parent's, as authors do in KBC-049), optionally linked to an author; `media_voice_labels` maps (media, diarization version, voice) to a speaker or to the special "audience question" speaker. Names can be suggested from context by the AI proposal (KBC-067: the media's author, vocatives, self-introductions, who answers most) without using the voice.
   - **As built (KBC-065):** the audience is a flag on the label, not a row; a label belongs to one separation (`diarization_id`), and a joined voice keeps its row with `merged_into`, so a join can be undone without touching the turns. A kept voice with no name takes the joined voice's name, marked `name_from_join`, so the undo gives it back what it had; a name a person gives it afterwards stays. A new separation carries names by time overlap (≥ 70 % of the old voice inside the new one) with `names_pending` set: listeners read those names only after a person confirms. Every change names the separation's version and a stale one answers 409. The subtitles file stays the same for everybody: the web adds the speaker to the cues from the transcript's segments.
3. **Voice profiles only with consent (KBC-071).** A `voice_profiles` row (speaker, collection, model, sealed embedding, ADR 0017) exists only with a `voice_consents` row recorded first (who, when, how, purpose, evidence); revoking deletes the profile and its embeddings. Profiles are compared on the Mac during `diarize` and sent to the worker only for the job's collection, deleted when the job ends; the browser never receives an embedding. Matches are suggestions; a person confirms. Who may have a profile follows the owner's decision 1.
4. **An edit is a versioned draft until a person approves it.** Nothing is cut, applied or shown to listeners before the person validates the tracks (listens, drags each start and end, fixes) and presses "Aprovar faixas"; AI proposals are draft tracks like any other. `media_edits` (media, version, status draft|approved, current step of the guided flow, updated by, `If-Match` etag) and `media_tracks` (edit, ordinal, start ms, end ms snapped to word edges or silence midpoints within 300 ms, or free after a ±0.5 s nudge, kind `qa|talk`, title, asker speaker, answerer speaker, source `manual|ai`, accepted, derived media id). Tracks never overlap (adjacent tracks may touch) and last at least 5 s, enforced by the editor and again by the API; gaps are allowed. Approved tracks are first served as chapters of the original (KBC-066: list, slider marks, current title, `?track=`). The validation timeline draws a waveform from peaks (10 per second) computed by the VM media worker in a `waveform` job after transcoding, with a backfill for older media.
5. **Approved tracks become derived media items beside the original (KBC-068).** Each approved track gets a `media` row with `parent_media_id`, `parent_start_ms`, `parent_end_ms`, inheriting collection and permissions from the parent. Its rendition is cut from the parent's AAC rendition by the VM media worker with `ffmpeg -ss … -to … -c copy` (no re-encode; packet precision ≈ 23 ms; real times recorded), `+faststart`. Its transcript is a derived version sliced from the parent's current transcript (provider `derived`, times shifted), so subtitles, word highlight, search and offline work unchanged. Re-approving matches old and new tracks by ≥ 70 % overlap to keep ids, positions, favourites and playlists; removed tracks are archived, never hard-deleted while referenced. The original stays (owner's decision 2).
   - **Editing after approval (KBC-073).** A track's id never changes on any edit (title, summary, speakers, subjects, bounds), so favourites, playlists, queues, shares and links survive. New bounds are re-cut from the full recording's rendition (stream copy) and swapped atomically; each re-cut bumps the track's version (`ETag`), so offline copies (ADR 0026) are replaced on the next contact and never mixed. Positions and bookmarks are stored relative to the track and remapped through the absolute time in the full recording (`absolute = old start + position`); a bookmark that falls outside moves to the full recording at the same absolute time. Deleting a track archives it for 30 days (restorable); merging and splitting outside a full re-edit are deferred.
   - **The full recording is never deleted while it has active tracks**; removing it requires removing the tracks first or an explicit cascade that lists them. Video is out until KBC-035 (stream copy of H.264 cuts only at keyframes).
6. **Subjects live in `media.db`, referencing the vault read-only.** `media_subject_links` (media id, subject kind `concept|free`, concept name as the catalog resolves it or a `media_subjects` row, source `manual|ai`, accepted, by, at) and a per-track summary. The vault is never written (Q7); a concept that disappears from the vault leaves the link flagged, not deleted. Only accepted links feed search (ADR 0024 §2 gains the concept filter for media) and the concept panel.
7. **AI over transcripts only, under the media cap.** Q&A detection, titles, context speaker suggestions and subject suggestions send transcript text (never audio) to the provider the master chose, in windows when needed, with schema-validated output; each run is estimated, reserved against the media monthly ledger (ADR 0022 §7, US$ 10) with a per-run cap of US$ 0.25 (owner's decision 3), refunded on failure, and runs only on a person's request. If KBC-063 shows a local model on the Mac is good enough, the same contract runs as a Mac job (`propose`) at no cost.
8. **One guided journey, as steps of the upload** (owner, 2026-11-03: "isso seria um passo do input de audios"): 1) upload and choose what to do (transcribe, who is speaking, tracks: none, manual or AI, subjects); 2) processing with detailed progress and the tracks appearing; 3) validate the tracks; 4) approve, which applies, cuts and classifies. A per-media preparation plan in `media.db` holds the requested steps, their state and the current step, so a person can leave and resume at the same step; step 3 is skipped only when no split was requested (KBC-072, KBC-066). The transcriber's heartbeat gains an optional progress field (additive to ADR 0023's protocol).
9. **Permissions, one per feature:** `media-edit` (voices, speakers, tracks), `media-speakers` (see who speaks), `media-tracks` (navigate and list tracks), `media-edit-ai` (AI proposals), `media-classify` (subjects), `media-subjects` (subjects in search and library), `media-voice-profiles` (masters by default).

## Options considered

1. **Tracks as derived media items cut by stream copy (chosen).** Every library feature (favourites, playlists, queue, offline, Media Session duration, search hits with their own title) works unchanged; storage grows by about the size of the parent; no quality loss.
2. **Tracks only as chapters (time ranges) of the original.** No extra storage and no cutting, but every feature would need to understand a sub-range (resume, duration, offline, playlists, search), spreading special cases through the player and the library; kept as the first step (KBC-066) and for the "listen in context" view.
3. **Re-encode each track.** Exact boundaries, but costs CPU on the 1-CPU worker and loses quality for no gain at speech bitrates.
4. **Replace the original by the tracks.** Simpler library, but the gaps (applause, intervals) and the continuity of the lecture would be lost, and with originals not kept the cut cannot be undone.
5. **Subject links in the vault (frontmatter of a transcript document).** Would make `kb` see them, but breaks Q7 and ADR 0024 and waits for a manual sync.
6. **Store voice embeddings for every recording to enable later identification.** Convenient, but creates biometric data of people who never agreed, including audience members; rejected.

## Consequences

- Good: the edit flow (voices → names → tracks → validate and approve → subjects) is incremental; each step is useful alone, and AI only proposes.
- Good: no biometric data exists without consent; per-recording voices are discarded after the job.
- Bad: more tables and three new job kinds; the janitor, backup snapshot and migrations grow.
- Bad: storage roughly doubles for edited recordings (29.6 MB/h → ≈ 59 MB/h); fine within the 100 GB volume at the planned volumes, revisit with the R2 move (ADR 0022).
- Bad: track boundaries are approximate by one AAC packet.
- Revisit when: KBC-063 changes the diarization or proposal approach; video editing is requested; the owner decides to hide or replace originals.
- Compliance: migration tests; integration tests for overlap and minimum-length validation (editor and API), nothing cut before approval, re-approval matching, archive on removal, derived transcripts and search; a test that no embedding is persisted without a consent row and that revocation deletes it; the vault hash unchanged in every edit test (Q7).
