# 0041 — Adding content from one page: inclusions with a ceiling, folders, recordings from links and lists, and a web search that goes on, all through the publishing Mac

- Keeps: Q7 (the API never writes the vault), [0036](0036-access-levels-per-collection.md) §5, [0017](0017-ai-keys-sealed-at-rest.md) (the Firecrawl key), [0033](0033-resumable-uploads-tus-core-on-the-item.md)
- Drivers: the owner's request of 2026-11-25 ("todo o mecanismo … de criar acervo e adicionar documentos (texto, áudio, pesquisa na web em vídeos e etc) … totalmente pronto no app"), and what they said while trying it: whole folders with nothing lost, an estimate before sending, a search that is asked for a number of sources with no limit, goes on, and never repeats what it gave, and a screen that says what is happening

## Context

Documents and links could be sent (ADR 0040), but there was no page for it, a folder or a channel could not be given, nothing bounded what one inclusion spends, and searching the web for sources was done in the terminal. The collection made on the screen had no folder in the vault until its first recording.

## Decision

1. **One page, `/adicionar?acervo=<slug>`**, for files and whole folders, links, the web search, and audio or video. `ContentSender` is app-wide: the sends go on while the person uses the app, the browser asks before the tab closes, and a new version of the app does not reload over them. Its account always closes (sent + already there + failed + on their way = total; a failure taken off the list is still said).
2. **An inclusion (`batch`) has a ceiling.** Each item carries `{id, capUsd}` (default US$ 0.50, 0.05 to 100, adjustable later). A lease takes the rest of the month's or the inclusion's, whichever is smaller, less what other leases hold; under `MIN_LEASE_USD` the item waits, said with `heldBy`. What an item spent still counts after it leaves the list. All spend is counted: vision, `kb ingest`, classify, link and the map step.
3. **The estimate comes from what happened here**: the 90th percentile of the last items of each kind (document, image, page, recording), first measurements until there are some.
4. **Recordings from links.** yt-dlp on the Mac takes the sound of one item (`--playlist-items 1 --max-filesize 2G --match-filter !is_live`, its own configuration ignored, a closed list of extractors: any other one, the generic or one that takes any host reached through a site's own forwarding page, would follow an address into this Mac's network; a podcast's episode, on its publisher's server, is fetched by this Mac as any other file) outside the vault lock, and gives it back as the sender's recording. A channel or playlist is listed first (`--flat-playlist`), and only what the person chooses becomes items. A video file enters by its sound. An audio or video file's address is fetched whole or not at all: public addresses only, each redirect checked, 2 GB and an hour at most for all of it, headers included. What a list holds is a request of its own (up to 5,000), never in the list of what is being done. There is no video player (the owner's choice). A recording whose publication failed is whole in the library: it goes again from its own place in the queue, never from its link.
5. **The web search is Firecrawl's**, with the house's key sealed like the AI keys. One round is one query of ten per kind and author; each next round asks in other words, an audio or a video of one site at a time. Names go unquoted: spelled another way a quoted one finds nothing. What neither names the author nor is about the subject (half of its words, by their roots, in the title, extract or address) is dropped, and so are social networks, shops, streaming players and the engine's own redirects as texts. The closest come first. A search is kept in memory by person, collection and request: the same request goes on from there, with what was found and not given yet first. What a person was given is remembered per collection as short hashes (`web-search-seen.json`), so another day's search leaves it out; `again` brings it back. The API gives up to 30 a request and starts no round after 20 s; a round nobody answered says nothing of what is left, and ends nothing; a wording the engine never answers is set apart while others are asked, and let go once they answer. Past the words each kind has of its own, a round takes one more from the titles found (the one most of them have), so a search has no last round while new things come: it ends when two rounds in a row bring nothing new; the screen asks until it has the number wanted, with no ceiling, and can be stopped.
6. **Documents before recordings.** A paired transcriber takes sent documents first (a minute each), five in a row, then what else waits has its turn. The screen says what else the Mac is doing, and no start time while it is.
7. **`kb index --embed=false`, then `qmd embed -c <collection>`.** `kb index` embeds every collection's pending documents; after a reconversion that is thousands, and one document would wait an hour for them.
8. **What kb refuses is decided on its own row.** The Mac gives back kb's reason and how the text begins; who sent it reads both, and a master puts it in all the same (`kb review accept` on the Mac, which spends nothing, then the usual steps) or leaves it out of the list: in kb's quarantine, nothing is deleted. kb's other review queues (links, contradictions, what it judged off topic but kept) stay in the terminal. A page kb refused at a recording's address goes again as a recording.
9. **Room.** A person's waiting files are bounded (5 GiB; not a master's) and the disk keeps a reserve (5 GiB). Bytes that stopped arriving are given up in a week; what ended leaves the record in three months.

## Consequences

- The Mac does the work: an item sent to an API no paired Mac talks to waits, and the screen says there is no Mac. The owner's Mac is paired with the local app (staging); production needs a pairing of its own.
- The search's relevance is lexical, in the words the engine shows: an ambiguous subject ("cocriação" in marketing and in mysticism) is told apart by its authors or by a longer subject.
- A search costs two Firecrawl credits a query. Measured on 2026-11-25: 30 sources for four authors, 18 credits; 57 over two requests without authors, 24.
- Measured on the owner's Mac on 2026-11-25, on a clone of the vault: a web page ready in 36 to 52 s for US$ 0.0002 to 0.0013; a five-minute recording downloaded, transcribed and published in about two minutes.
